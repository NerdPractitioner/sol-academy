# Nerd Practitioner — Brand Brief (v0.1)

## The name
**Nerd Practitioner** is a play on "nurse practitioner." A practitioner is someone who *practices*: nerdy passions are skills you build by practicing, not a phase you grow out of.

## Mission
Help kids and adults who were told their nerdy passions "don't pay" treat those passions as a real craft and a real path, with enough business sense to make it last.

## Who it's for
- Nerds who grew up being discouraged from games, comics, art, code or anime-style storytelling because "you can't make money doing that."
- Especially people with **ADHD and social anxiety**: big ideas and deep focus, but it's hard to start, finish or put yourself out there.
- Groups of friends (around 5 people) who love getting good at a game together.

## Brand pillars
1. **Practice > talent.** Show the reps, the failures and the progress. Nobody starts out good.
2. **Nerd + business.** Every project also shows how it earns: pricing, wishlists, audience, money. That knowledge is the empowering part.
3. **Together.** Games and content that bring small groups of friends together. Co-op, shared victories, hanging out and shooting the breeze.
4. **Action-sized steps.** ADHD-friendly: small concrete next steps, quick wins, visible progress, and nothing that relies on willpower alone.

## Voice
Warm, funny, a little chaotic, honest about struggle. It sounds like a friend who's a few steps ahead, not a guru. Encouraging without being fake: it shares the real numbers, including the bad ones.

## What the brand makes
| Line | Examples | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Games** | The space-academy JRPG (first title); later co-op/survival games for friend groups | Flagship product, main revenue |
| **Content** | YouTube devlogs, Twitch streams (building + playing co-op/survival games with friends) | Audience, wishlists, community |
| **Comics & art** | Game-world comics, character art, merch | Brand world, extra revenue |
| **Teaching** | Later: a course / guide for small-creator game dev with ADHD in mind (model: Thomas Brush) | Revenue + the mission itself |
| **Community** | Discord: accountability streams, "practice sessions," co-op game nights | The "together" pillar |

## How the pieces feed each other
Build the game → film the building (devlog) → audience wishlists the game → the game sells → teach what worked → students become the community → community playtests the next game.

## Revenue ladder (rough order)
1. Steam game sales (first real income)
2. YouTube ad revenue + Twitch subs (slow, compounding)
3. Merch / comics
4. Course or paid community (once there's a track record worth teaching)
